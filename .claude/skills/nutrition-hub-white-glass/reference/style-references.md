## What each one does

**R1: talking head that dives into graphics.** The speaker sits on a stool against a navy wall.
- A tall glass card stands *behind* the speaker, with text on it ("You are Growing"). This needs the person cut out.
- A defocus blur carries the cut into full-screen graphic beats:
  - a glass icon tile with an arrow that flips from up-blue to down-red
  - a 3D moss sphere ringed by task chips ("Update spreadsheet", "Generate invoice")
- A defocus blur carries it back to the speaker, with a big bold word ("costs") and a small emoji sticker.
- The key word is in an accent italic script.
- Pace: a new beat about every 1 s.

**R2: faceless kinetic type, white stage.** One continuous camera with no cuts. Each idea builds and then the camera moves on.
- **Words build one at a time.** "Attention" lands, then "is", then "expensive" types on letter by letter in the accent colour.
  - "Attention" comes in from about 2.5× scale with motion blur and settles in about 0.3 s. That's a slam, banned here; see the decisions below.
- **Objects:**
  - a clock hand that swings in
  - viewfinder corner brackets framing people icons, with one person in ink and the rest in the accent colour
  - a countdown ring ("7")
  - hexagon icon nodes with thin leader lines and small labels ("Losing prospects / momentum / opportunities")
- **Pill chips build into a list:** filled accent pills alternate with outline pills ("They're clearer", "Sharper", "They remove friction").
- **Closing line** slides in horizontally ("Because clarity isn't an advantage. It's leverage."). An accent outline pill draws around the final phrase.
- **Depth:** large soft accent rings, blurred, in the corners.
- **Pace:** a new element about every 0.4–0.8 s; each idea holds about 1–1.5 s.

**R3: faceless kinetic type with photo metaphors.** White stage, near-black type, one mustard accent.
- **Type** mixes a bold sans, a condensed display face ("PEOPLE", "CARING.", "OWN MINDS") and an italic serif accent word ("the", "Same thing", "indifference").
- **Realistic cut-out photos as metaphors:**
  - a bored person on a conveyor of boxes labelled SAME
  - a "Same / New Narrative" signpost
  - a brain held in hands
  - top-down people around a phone
- **UI and graphic devices:**
  - a stacked card carousel ("Reveal it / **Build curiosity** / mystery") with the off-focus cards blurred
  - a highlighter bar that sweeps behind a phrase ("Let people wonder")
  - connect-the-dots lines radiating from a portrait
  - corner brackets
- It opens on concentric rings and has one black flash frame near the end.
- It ends on the creator's logo.

**R4: talking head + frosted UI.** One take of the speaker in a chair, light warm-grey wall, black outfit.
- **Above the head:** frosted white cards simulate the story. These include:
  - a "We do performance marketing" poster card and a list of "Leads"
  - a call log ("No answer", "Calling…"), a ₹73,000 cost bar, and the "Think → Work → Process" steps
  - a chat bubble typing "Hey! let's conn|"
- **Beside the speaker:** a kinetic caption block of small grey words stacked around one big bold word ("Agree", "Leads", "Browsing", "Convincing", "Reached"). It changes about every 0.5–1 s, on the voice.
- The cards never cover the face, and the type sits in the empty space beside the speaker.

**R5: talking head with two caption tiers and depth.** A speaker sits in an armchair in a bright, real living room (shallow depth of field).
- **Tier 1, the headline behind the head.** Vertical centre about 25–35%, centred on the speaker.
  - Small lowercase lead words (white, about 0.35× size) sit above-left of a big bold key word: "that most / **sellers**", "the way your / **HOME**", "because / **2014**", "just / **2 YEARS**", "**$40 MILLION**".
  - The speaker's head covers the middle letters, so the word sits behind her. This needs a person cut-out.
  - Each headline word gets its own fill: teal, maroon-to-orange gradient, gold metallic or grey.
- **Tier 2, the running caption on the chest.** One small bold white word at a time ("makes", "career", "considering"), sometimes two lines ("at least / curious", "In / Real Estate").
  - Measured: about 27 px tall on 1080×1920, horizontal centre 49%, vertical centre 51% (middle half of all readings: 47–56%).
  - It sits at collarbone height. No box, a soft shadow only.
- **Other moves:**
  - jump cuts to a tighter crop on emphasis words
  - b-roll cutaways through a white flash
  - an Instagram profile card, app screenshots, fanned photo cards, chrome spheres orbiting
- **Pace:** a shot about every 4 s; the caption word changes with every spoken word.

## Captions: arrangement, font styles and placement (measured)

Measured by sampling each reel 3 times a second and reading every word's box with OCR
(Tesseract). Positions are on a 1080×1920 frame. Font names are the closest match by eye, not
confirmed.

### Where the text sits

| Ref | 0–15% | 15–35% | 35–55% | 55–75% | 75–100% | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R4 talking head | 9% | 50% | 41% | 0% | 0% | **Two slots only**, never below the chest. See below. |
| R1 talking head | 1% | 54% | 9% | 35% | 0% | Big word above the head. The lower text comes from the full-screen graphic scenes, not over the speaker. |
| R2 faceless | 0% | 22% | 60% | 5% | 13% | One line on the **exact vertical centre** (y 50%). Small supporting lines sit low, around 75–85%. |
| R3 faceless | 2% | 32% | 42% | 21% | 4% | Type-only beats are centred around y 47–52%. With a photo, the headline moves to a top-left block (y 20–30%) and the image fills the middle. |
| R5 talking head | 0% | 36% | 28% | 27% | 9% | **Two tiers**: a headline behind the head at about 25–35%, and one small word on the chest at about 51%. (The lower bands include text on photo cards and b-roll.) |

**R4's two caption slots (the talking-head system):**
1. **Beside the head, left side.** The big word's left edge is at about x 120 px (11% of the width).
   Its vertical centre is 35–42%, at eye-to-shoulder height, in the empty wall left of the face.
2. **Top band.** Vertical centre 15–20%, used for sentence-ending lines ("Performance Marketing.") and
   whenever the left side is taken by a UI card. The UI cards also sit in the top 5–30%, top-left or top-right.
3. Nothing ever goes over the face, the hands or the body (0% of text below 55%).

### How a caption is built (R4, the clearest system)

- **One big key word per phrase.** It's in title case, a heavy neo-grotesk (it looks like SF Pro Display or Inter Display at weight 800–900), with tight tracking (about −3 to −4%) and solid ink.
  - It's 75–150 px tall on 1080×1920.
- **Small connector words tucked around it**, lowercase in a regular or light *italic* sans:
  - grey, about 0.32× the big word's height (range 0.14–0.49×)
  - placed **above-left** ("and I / **Agree**", "someone / **Finds**", "what / **Comes**") or
    **below-right** ("**Browsing** / they were", "**Agree** / with that"), sometimes overlapping its edge.
- No box, stroke or shadow; dark type straight on the light wall.
- The big word changes on the voice, about **once a second** (median gap 1.0 s). Small words appear just before or after it.
- Sentence end: one full sentence in bold, sentence case with a full stop, in the top band.

## Adapt first

| Technique (ref) | Change needed |
|---|---|
| Stacked card carousel with blurred off-focus cards (R3) | Fine as glass cards. Use glass-reveal timing, with no bounce when a card becomes the focused one. |
| Realistic photo metaphors (R3) and the 3D sphere (R1) | Real photos or `templates/molecule-3d.html` / `product-hero-3d.html` only. Never AI-generated packaging, labels, logos or people's bodies (§4, §10). |
| Defocus blur into a full-screen graphic and back (R1) | Allowed as a soft cross-dissolve with a light blur (0.3–0.5 s). Keep the white stage. |
| Text behind the speaker (R1, R5) | Needs a person cut-out (the IT Reelsmaker plugin can cut out the person). Keep the face and eyes clear, and keep enough of the word showing to read it. |
| Jump cut to a tighter crop on an emphasis word (R5) | A hard cut between about 100% and 115–120% framing, never an animated zoom punch (§5). At most one every 4–6 s, and the reframe stays sharp (1080p source or better). |
| A different colour fill per headline word (R5: teal, maroon-orange, gold metallic) | One accent only: ink headline words, with the claim or number word in orange. No gradients or metallic fills. |

## Leave out (conflicts with White Glass)

- **Dark navy, red and lime full-screen scenes (R1):** no dark backgrounds or extra hues (§2).
- **Motion-blur scale slams and whip pushes (R2's "Attention" entrance, its clock whip):** banned by §5 (no zoom punches, no whip pans).
- **Pixel-block glitch reveals (R1)** and the **black flash frame (R3):** banned by §5.
- **A second accent colour:** these refs each use one accent, and so do we. Ours is orange.

## Open decisions for the owner

These refs use type and motion that White Glass currently rules out. They change only if the owner says so:

1. **An italic serif for the accent word** (R2, R3, R4: "expensive", "every second", "indifference").
   White Glass is Inter only. If adopted, pick one free family (for example Instrument Serif or
   Playfair Display italic), one word per line at most, always in orange.
2. **A condensed display face for one-word slams** (R3: "PEOPLE", "OWN MINDS"). Currently Inter 800 instead.
3. **Faster entrances.** The refs land words with a short motion blur. White Glass would allow a
   0.25–0.35 s ease-out with a slight blur and no scale overshoot, but not R2's 2.5× slam.
4. **Bare captions over footage (R5).** R5's chest captions are white text with only a soft shadow.
   White Glass puts every word over footage on a panel, so the chest line above uses a small white pill.
   Bare text is possible if the owner prefers the R5 look, with a stronger soft shadow for legibility.

## Pacing targets taken from the refs

- Hook word or line readable by about 0.5 s.
- A new element about every 0.5–1 s inside a beat; each idea holds about 1–1.5 s.
- Talking-head pieces: the caption block changes on the voice, about every 0.5–1 s, with one bold word per change.
- These run faster than §5's "beat every 1.5–3 s". Use §5 for explainers that need reading time, and these targets for punchy social reels.
